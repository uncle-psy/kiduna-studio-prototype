The illustration is a combination of two scenes from Jacobus Sulat's Mutus liber in quo tamen tota Philosophia hermetica, figures hieroglyphicis depingitur (La Rochelle, 1677). The pictures show different stages in the alchemical process, performed by male and female alchemists. Collected dew is distilled and separated into four coagulated particles. The result of further distillations is conjoined with an extract concentrated by the secret lunar fire.

The "philosophical egg." or mercurial "vessel of nature," is prepared and sealed. The first scene shows the two alchemists pouring collected dew into a large pan and heating it over an athanor. In the second scene the 159 The Cards female is stirring the fluid in the pan held by the male. In her left hand, she holds an alembic, or flask, into which four ingredients have been dropped. In the third scene the male is weighing what was in the alembic and adding material from a pewter jug. The female combines ingredients and pours them into another glass flask.

In the final scene both alchemists are working together to pour what is possibly saltpeter into a flask, which will later be given to Mercury, who needs it for the crystallization of his inner, sulfurous germ of "spiritual gold." ## Card description

Four sequential workshop scenes show a male and female alchemist cooperating. First they pour collected dew into a large pan over an athanor. Next the woman stirs while holding an alembic containing four ingredients. Then the man weighs the material and adds from a pewter jug while the woman combines ingredients into another flask. Finally they work together to pour saltpeter into the vessel that will be given to Mercury for crystallization of the inner sulphurous germ of spiritual gold.
