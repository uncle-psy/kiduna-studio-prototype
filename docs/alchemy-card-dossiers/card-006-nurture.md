## Complete narrative

Nurture is the keyword for this card.

The picture contains the element of one, the priest, and two, the mother and child. In nurturing, sharing, providing, you are acting the part of the goddess, in distributing your blessings among others. There is no need for any formalization of religion for this to take place. It is something which you can, and should, do in everyday life. However little, you may feel you have, it invariably have more than many others. Always give thanks for what you have, but then share with those less fortunate.

You have a tendency to fight authority, in many instances, you feel there is too much red tape, and frequently your instincts are correct in wanting to bypass it. But some form of law, and legislation is necessary. So there are times when you have to bite the bullet, and follow the rules. Try to develop patience for such times. Though much formalized religion is designed to give the priesthood power over the masses, there are those who are sincere in their devotions, and delight in the formalities and ceremonies. Encourage these to tread the path they have chosen.

Extend your love to them, and send them on their way with your blessings. One is the number of the driving life force, yet the priest, here the number one, is so driving that he loses sight of his direction. He automatically assumes command, even, when not entitled to. There is a tendency to be impatient. Two is a sensitive, domestic number, associated with the moon, as evidenced by the mother and child sitting on the crescent. It is the perfect couple of mother and child. The blue of the mother's dress is the blue of tranquility, patience, and understanding. It is also the blue of health.

The red of the crescent is the color of vigor, of courage, health, and love. Beyond the couple, rise the flames of the golden aura, protection, attraction, and confidence.

Planetary conjunction: Venus, Mars. These are two powerful planets, and often, it is not easy to determine, which is the stronger of the two. Here it is Venus, resulting in a softening effect, leading to the emphasis on nurturing. The sex drive is powerful, with the female not afraid to take the initiative. A tendency to promiscuity should be watched, but otherwise sex may be enjoyed, and the sex life will probably be an active one. There is a tendency to expect too much of others, and consequently, to be disappointed when they do not live up to your expectations. They may well be difficult.

There may well be difficulties in trying to establish long-lasting partnerships.

Stone: rose quartz. This is a stone associated with love, both physical and spiritual. It is a stone that eases tensions and gives self-confidence. It is also a stone for creativity. This makes it a stone, much favored by artists of all types. The rose quartz is certainly a stone that will aid, the nurturing principle. Worn as a pendant, over the heart, it can have powerful influences. Quartz itself is associated with the moon. In this card, we see the goddess seated on a crescent moon. Grade: The card is, in the Initiate grade. You have traveled a distance along the path and are no longer a beginner. This means, you have responsibilities. You already have experience on which you may draw, yet bear in mind, that you do not, yet know all that needs to be known. You still have much to learn. Carefully guard these secrets you have uncovered, for they will stand you in good stead in the time to come. The symbol for this, grade is the balance. Balance then, what you know, against what you need to know. Acknowledge your in-norance while enjoying your knowledge

## Card description

a priest before an altar in a church. He is conducting a ritual. Behind him in an alcove, sits a mother nursing a child. They are seated on a crescent moon, and enclosed in a flaming aura. The priest would seem to be absorbed in his devotions, which are performed by rote, to the point where he is totally unaware of the presence of divinity behind him. The figure with a child is Mother Earth, the goddess herself. In the alchemical text, the scene is presented as earth, suckling the mercurius child. Matrix, aeas terra est. From the crescent on, which they sit, It is as much the moon goddess as it is Mother Earth, although the two are aspects of one another. The ancient Persians called the upturned crescent the Horn of Plenty. Certainly the goddess pru-provides plenty of sustenance for humankind.
