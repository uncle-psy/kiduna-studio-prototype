# Gulf War Illness — The Invisible Load

**Semantic kernel:** A varied cluster of chronic symptoms can remain real and consequential even when one cause, test, or universal case definition does not contain every Veteran's experience.

## Terminology

VA uses **Gulf War illness**, **chronic multisymptom illness**, **medically unexplained chronic multisymptom illness**, and **undiagnosed illness**. VA prefers not to use **Gulf War Syndrome** because reported symptoms vary widely. This card keeps the user's familiar term as an alias but uses current official terminology in the graph.

## Grounded anchor

VA describes symptom clusters that may include fatigue, headaches, joint pain, indigestion, insomnia, dizziness, respiratory disorders, and memory problems. Current VA benefits pages describe specified presumptive pathways for qualifying service, locations, duration, conditions, and other criteria.

## Moving toward

Recognition, individualized clinical evaluation, current official navigation, symptom-specific care, participant credibility, qualified research, and clear separation among health, registry, and benefits processes.

## Moving away

Dismissal because causation is uncertain; diagnosis by card; one-cause certainty; one-treatment certainty; stereotypes about Gulf War Veterans; or outdated eligibility language presented as current law.

## Sense → Choose → Act → Learn

- **Sense:** Separate symptoms, diagnoses, exposure concerns, research definitions, benefits rules, participant statements, and unknowns.
- **Choose:** Consider speaking with a primary care provider or VA environmental-health coordinator, using the Gulf War Registry, or seeking current benefits help as appropriate.
- **Act:** Route to qualified care or official/accredited help. The card cannot diagnose, treat, or determine service connection.
- **Learn:** Preserve what helped, what remained unresolved, and what the participant consents to contribute. One experience does not become the cohort.
